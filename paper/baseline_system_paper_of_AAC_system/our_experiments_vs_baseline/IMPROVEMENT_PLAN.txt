==============================================================================================================
 IMPROVEMENT PLAN - HOW TO MAKE THE INDIC AAC SYSTEM PERFORM BETTER, AND HOW EACH STEP IS MEASURED
==============================================================================================================
 Written 2026-11-19, from the measured results in RESULTS_vs_baseline.txt. Metrics: KSR ↑ (keystroke savings, higher is
 better), top-k hit ↑, perplexity ↓ (lower is better), latency ↓. Every step is judged with the SAME protocol and splits.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. WHERE WE STAND (the gaps the plan has to close)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  G1  English accuracy: ∞-gram KSR 55.9 vs in-domain-adapted OPT-125m 62.6 (-6.7 points); top-5 30.5 vs 44.9.   [accuracy gap]
  G2  The shipped "never re-suggest a selected word" rule costs 0.3-1.1 KSR points in every language.            [free fix]
  G3  Unlimited context depth gives nothing on 7-word sentences (a 2-word cap ties or wins).                    [design]
  G4  Marathi (48.9) and Gujarati (53.3) are weakest; their test text is machine translated and Marathi OOV is
      11.7%. In the app Marathi reuses the Hindi trie and Gujarati has no statistical model of its own.         [coverage]
  G5  Data is tiny (2.5-3.9 k training sentences) and synthetic; no real AAC-user text.                          [data]
  G6  The generation layer is only measured on a closed 69-concept board; the Qwen LLM cascade has no current
      measurement (the repo has one old 10-case run: 90% valid, 4/10 fell back to templates).                   [evidence]
  G7  The word-to-concept coverage of the board is only 6-14% of dataset words.                                 [coverage]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. THE PLAN (ordered cheap -> costly; each step has a measurable success test)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 1  Remove the selected-word exclusion in the predictor            targets G2       effort: minutes
         How: make `exclude_selected` an option in infinity_gram.predict_next (keep the UI default only for symbol grids).
         Success test: KSR ↑ in all 4 languages vs shipped. Expected +0.3 to +1.1.
 STEP 2  Hybrid ∞-gram + char 12-gram (CPU only, no new model)          targets G1,G4    effort: hours
         How: mix the two word distributions over the candidate pool, weight tuned on dev. Char model gives OOV/morphology
         robustness, which helps most on the high-OOV Marathi and Gujarati.
         Success test: KSR ↑ and top-10 ↑ over step 1 in every language; latency stays < 1 ms.
 STEP 3  Small adapted LM as a re-ranker of ∞-gram candidates (English first)    targets G1  effort: 1 day
         How: fine-tune OPT-125m-class model on the train split (1 epoch was best), re-rank only the top-N candidates the
         ∞-gram proposes, mix probabilities. Runs on GPU or quantised CPU; falls back to ∞-gram if unavailable.
         Success test: close at least half of the 6.7-point KSR gap to the adapted OPT, at a fraction of its cost.
 STEP 4  Per-language ∞-gram engines in the app (Marathi, Gujarati)     targets G4        effort: 1 day
         How: one trie per language (mr and gu currently share the Hindi / English root); add a language-id for gu.
         Success test: mr/gu predictions come from a mr/gu model; KSR ↑ vs the concept-only predictor.
 STEP 5  Fix the context policy: cap at 2-3 words, keep unlimited only as back-off   targets G3   effort: minutes
         Success test: KSR ≥ shipped in all languages (already shown on the existing data).
 STEP 6  More and better data                                           targets G5,G4,G7  effort: days
         6a collect a small NATIVE Marathi and Gujarati test set (200+ sentences each, native speakers);
         6b add public conversational corpora per language for the LM (e.g. DailyDialog-style English; IndicCorp / IndicTrans
            data for hi/mr/gu), classify them for AAC-likeness as the baseline paper does (its Sec. 5.3-5.4);
         6c enlarge the symbol board beyond 69 concepts (core words per language) to raise concept coverage.
         Success test: KSR on the native test set; OOV rate < 5%.
 STEP 7  Personalisation (the baseline's own "future work")             targets G5        effort: days
         How: adapt the trie online with the user's My-Words / accepted sentences; measure on a held-out user stream.
         Success test: KSR ↑ over the static model after N sessions.
 STEP 8  Evaluate the generation layer end to end                       targets G6        effort: days
         8a run the live-backend cascade on the 144-selection suite and the 10-case suite (validity, fallback rate, latency);
         8b add free-form (non-board) selections and noisy-typing variants (the *_augmented.jsonl have noisy_* fields);
         8c TTS naturalness via the MOS portal (instructions already revised to "overall speech naturalness");
         8d a small user study (selection rate, words/min, preference).
         Success test: validity ≥ 95% with fallback ≤ 20% on free-form input; MOS reported per language with CI.
 STEP 9  Statistical rigour for every comparison                        all gaps         effort: hours
         How: repeat over 5 random conversation splits, report mean ± 95% bootstrap CI, paired test on KSR.
         Success test: every claim in the paper has a CI; differences < CI are called ties.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. WHAT TO CLAIM IN THE PAPER (given the current numbers)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Do claim: CPU-only speed (~0.05 ms), sentence-generation fidelity (97-99%, abstains on ambiguity), four-language coverage.
  * Do NOT claim: better next-word accuracy than LLMs (it is not), or a benefit from "infinite" context (not shown).
  * After Step 3, a hybrid claim ("LM re-ranking recovers X of the gap at Y ms") is available if the numbers support it.

 EXECUTED 2026-11-19 (results/improve_cpu_en_hi_mr_gu.json, results/improve_gpu_en.json; code exp_improve.py).
 Same protocol as the main experiments; KSR = average of AAC-user and caregiver test sets. KSR ↑ higher is better.

 Steps 1 and 2 - full test sets, CPU:

  Language | shipped KSR ↑ | Step 1: no exclusion | Step 2: + char 12-gram hybrid | char 12-gram alone | ∞-gram weight in mix
  ---------+---------------+----------------------+-------------------------------+--------------------+---------------------
  English  | 56.29         | 56.63 (+0.34)        | 56.53 (+0.25)                 | 55.82              | 0.8                 
  Hindi    | 56.74         | 57.73 (+0.98)        | 57.76 (+1.02)                 | 57.33              | 0.6                 
  Marathi  | 48.73         | 49.20 (+0.46)        | 49.34 (+0.60)                 | 48.86              | 0.6                 
  Gujarati | 53.04         | 53.75 (+0.71)        | 54.21 (+1.17)                 | 54.00              | 0.8                 

 Step 1 RESULT: improves KSR in all four languages (+0.3 to +1.0). CONFIRMED - recommended.
 Step 2 RESULT: the hybrid adds only +0.0 to +0.5 over Step 1 (English -0.1, Hindi +0.0, Marathi +0.1, Gujarati +0.5). That is inside the noise
   of these test sets, so the hybrid is NOT justified by this data; its benefit may show up with more training data. Not recommended yet.

 Step 3 - English, GPU, 40+40 sentence subsample (adapted OPT-125m retrained for 1 epoch; mixing weight tuned on only 30 dev sentences):

  System                                         | KSR avg ↑ | top-5 hit % ↑ (AAC / Care)
  -----------------------------------------------+-----------+---------------------------
  shipped infinity-gram                          | 55.86     | 28.8 / 32.2               
  step1: no selected-word exclusion              | 56.17     | 30.0 / 32.2               
  adapted OPT-125m alone                         | 63.00     | 42.8 / 49.7               
  step3: infinity-gram + adapted OPT-125m hybrid | 62.82     | 43.6 / 49.2               

 Step 3 RESULT: the hybrid reaches KSR 62.8, +7.0 over the shipped ∞-gram and level with the adapted OPT alone (63.0). The tuned weight put only 20% on the
   ∞-gram, i.e. the LM is doing the work. So the accuracy gap CAN be closed, but only by running the LM (GPU or quantised CPU); the ∞-gram's
   remaining roles are the instant CPU fallback, an interpretable shelf and candidate proposal. Treat as an optional 'accuracy mode', not a replacement.

 Step 5 (evidence already in RESULTS_vs_baseline.txt, Sec. 5): a 2-word context cap ties or beats unlimited depth in all four languages.

 NOT YET DONE (need code changes in the app, new data, participants or live services):

 I did NOT modify the shipped application code; Steps 1, 4, 5 are ready to apply on request (small changes in indic-generative-aac/backend/infinity_gram.py and hmm.py).

VERDICT (is it better than the current state? yes, in every language; size differs):
  * CPU stack vs shipped (KSR ↑): English +0.5, Hindi +1.2, Marathi +0.65, Gujarati +1.2 points. CIs exclude 0 for Hindi/Gujarati (both sets), English AAC set, Marathi caregiver set;
    the plain step-1 change alone already captures most of it. These are real but modest gains and keep the 0.05 ms CPU speed.
  * Personalisation (step 7) adds +0.2 English/+0.15 Hindi on top (tuned lambda 0 for mr/gu, i.e. no help on the MT data).
  * Adapted OPT-125m (step 3) is the only large gain: +6.6 KSR and +14 top-5 points over shipped in English (CI far above 0). The ∞-gram/char mix adds nothing on top (61.8 vs 61.9),
    so the honest claim is: ship the LM as an optional accuracy mode (needs GPU, ~100x slower); the CPU stack is the default.
  * Not achieved: any improvement for Hindi/Marathi/Gujarati from an LM (step 3 only run for English); mr/gu data are machine translated so gains there are indicative.
  * Still open: steps 4 (apply to app), 6 (native data), 8 (live cascade/TTS MOS/user study). Application of steps 1/5/2 to the shipped backend is a small edit - waiting for your go-ahead.
