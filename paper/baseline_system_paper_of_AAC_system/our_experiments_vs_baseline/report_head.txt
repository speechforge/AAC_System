 0. READ THIS FIRST - WHAT IS AND IS NOT COMPARABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * The baseline paper studies CHARACTER prediction for letter-by-letter AAC (Dasher, RSVP, Nomon ...), using
   pretrained OPT / GPT-2 / Llama LLMs, evaluated on private crowd-sourced English test sets with perplexity,
   keystroke savings (KSR, 5 word predictions) and latency.
 * Our system is SYMBOL / WORD based: the user taps words/symbols, a word-level infinity-gram (∞-gram) engine
   suggests next words, a constrained realiser (+ optional Qwen cascade) turns the selection into one sentence,
   a deterministic semantic gate checks it, and TTS speaks it. The two systems therefore overlap on exactly
   one task - predicting what the user wants to say next - and the baseline has no sentence generation, no
   validation, no TTS and no Indic language support at all.
 * I could NOT run the baseline's own models or data: its private test sets, its adapted OPT-350m, its 21B-char
   AAC n-gram and its Common-Crawl/subtitle adaptation corpora are not in this repo. Instead, I re-implemented
   the baseline's character-search algorithm (paper Sec. 3, verified: "yeste" -> r = 0.994, paper reports
   0.9999) and ran the SAME protocol on the SAME data for every system. Numbers are therefore NOT comparable
   with the absolute values printed in the paper (e.g. its perplexity 2.1-2.5); only the relative ordering of
   systems inside this report is meaningful.
 * Our corpus is small and synthetic (GPT-generated caregiver<->AAC-user dialogues, 277 English conversations,
   375 Hindi); Marathi and Gujarati have NO native corpus, so their test text is machine-translated (NLLB-200)
   from the English splits. Nothing here comes from real AAC users.
