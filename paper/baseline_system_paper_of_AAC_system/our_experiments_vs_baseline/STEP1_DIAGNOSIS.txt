FINDINGS
  F1 Ranking headroom is large everywhere: English 14.3, Hindi 12.3, Marathi 14.3, Gujarati 12.1 KSR points between current and a perfect ranker over the same pool. Ranking/LM quality, not the candidate pool, is the main lever -> supports STEP 2 (neural LM on the GPU).
     (English reference: adapted OPT-125m reached 61.9 vs 56.8 CPU stack = about 1/3 of the headroom; a perfect ranker is not attainable, so expect partial closure.)
  F2 Marathi is lower mostly because its CEILING is lower: oracle(pool) 63.7 vs Hindi 70.3 (avg AAC/Care). Cause = coverage: test OOV 14.2%/9.7% (Hindi 4.7%/2.5%), type/token 0.19 (Hindi 0.074), 58% of vocabulary seen once.
     Marathi headroom (14.4) is as large as English (14.3) - the model is not worse at ranking; the data are sparser and more inflected.
  F3 More data would help: Marathi OOV falls 22.9 -> 16.7 -> 11.7% as train grows 25 -> 50 -> 100% and is still dropping steeply. Gujarati 15.8 -> 10.9 -> 7.8%. We are data-bound, not model-bound.
  F4 About 36-41% of OOV tokens are inflected forms of known words, so a suffix-/character-aware fallback (STEP 4) can recover a large part of the OOV loss without new data.
  F5 The Marathi translations are the weakest of the three (round-trip chrF median 20.6 vs Gujarati 27.4, Hindi 40.2). Some Marathi KSR deficit may be translation noise; this needs native text before it is claimed.

IMPLICATIONS FOR THE NEXT STEPS (decision rules from IMPROVEMENT_PLAN_INDEPTH.txt Part 6 applied)
  1. Do STEP 2 now (neural LM adaptation on GPU for mr/gu): headroom >10 points, so the rule 'stop ranking work if headroom <10' does NOT trigger.
  2. Do STEP 3 in parallel for Marathi first (native/more data): coverage is its binding constraint.
  3. STEP 4 (morphology/char fallback) has a measured target: ~40% of OOV tokens are near-inflections.
  4. Any Marathi/Gujarati gain on the current MT data stays 'indicative' until a small native test set exists.
Limits of this diagnosis: single split; oracle assumes perfect top-1; chrF audit has no human raters; Hindi-vs-Marathi comparison is on different (translated) corpora.
