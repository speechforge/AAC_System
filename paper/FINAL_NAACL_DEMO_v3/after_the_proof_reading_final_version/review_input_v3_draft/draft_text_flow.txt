Symbol-based augmentative and alternative
communication (AAC) devices voice telegraphic labels, whereas Hindi, Gujarati, and
Marathi sentences need case marking, agreement, and negation. A language model can
expand a selection into a sentence, but it can
also replace a concept, drop a negation, or add
content, and an AAC device speaks for its user.
We present Indic AAC Studio, a web system
for English, Hindi, Gujarati, and Marathi that
speaks a model-generated sentence only if a
deterministic gate confirms the selected concepts, polarity, and intended act, and otherwise
speaks a template built from the user’s own
labels. On 175 frozen scenarios sent as the
web client builds them, the gate raises success
from 37% to 55% and keeps all 215 required
concept instances (the ungated cascade keeps
175/215) with no polarity error; with explicit
intent fields, success rises from 41% to 75%.
A template alone reaches 67% and 95%, so under a lexical scorer the gate is a safeguard and
not a quality gain. The failure audit produced a
repaired build, scored here only as a regression
check. A suffix-trie predictor exceeds trigram
backoff by 1.1 hit@1 points in English and 1.2
in Hindi. Speech synthesis is integrated but
was not evaluated; a reviewer package and a
screencast accompany the submission.

Feelings and negation use other constructions,
so a literal join of labels is hard to parse. A language model can generalize across combinations,
but the small model that fits a 4 GB GPU in our
setup, QWEN2.5-0.5B-INSTRUCT, produced a successful sentence for only 8/175 of our scenarios
when prompted without constraints (Section 4).
Even when fluent, the cascade evaluated here fails
in three ways (Figure 1): it replaced the concept
for Water, inverted polarity for Marathi No + Water,
and added content for Tired; with explicit negation,
it dropped or inverted polarity in 36/175 scenarios.
Indic AAC Studio treats the selection as a contract. A concept ledger lists the concepts that must
appear and those that must not; a prompted cascade
proposes a sentence; a deterministic gate accepts
it only if its checks on concepts, polarity, and communicative act pass; and otherwise a template built
from the user’s own labels is spoken. QWEN2.50.5B-INSTRUCT and INDICF5 are used as released.
Our contribution is the application and evaluation
of explicit concept, polarity, and act checks in an
Indic symbol-to-speech workflow, including the
consequences of its fallback policy, for symbolboard users and the caregivers and clinicians who
configure boards.
• Concept-gated realization: a ledger, a four-part
acceptance rule, retry, and a template floor (Section 3).
• A four-language demo with predictors trained
on AAC dialogues (Section 3, Appendix Table 7).
• A frozen, paired evaluation of 175 scenarios
and the audit-driven repair (Sections 4 and 5).

or No + Pizza) and the listener cannot see the selection, so a fluent sentence that misstates it can cause
harm when the message is a request, a refusal, or
a report of pain. For a water request, a single Water symbol can be realized as a complete sentence

Expanding telegraphic input. Compansion expanded telegraphic AAC input into well-formed
English sentences with syntactic and semantic
rules (McCoy et al., 1998). Recent systems apply language models to other inputs. SpeakFaster expands abbreviated text for eye-gaze typists (Cai et al., 2024); ConnecTone suggests text
from conversational context and speaks it with a
style-adaptive TTS (Francis et al., 2024); Mohan
et al. (2024) compose messages from typed, emoji,
or spoken input in the user’s own voice. Karmakar and Sinha (2024) contribute a symbol-totext dataset, a text-to-pictogram module, and an extension to Bengali. Indic AAC Studio starts from
symbol selections, which give the model a small
concept set whose omission or inversion can be
checked; its novelty lies in that check and its evaluation, not in the expansion itself.

RQ1: the gate preserves what the cascade loses.
Before the gate, the cascade keeps 81% of required
concepts under client payloads (86% with explicit
fields) and, with explicit fields, makes 36/175 polarity errors, 35 of them dropped negations (Table 2). With the gate and template floor, the same
requests keep all 215 concepts and make no polarity error on this benchmark; these counts describe what the implemented checks test, not a
guarantee of meaning. Success rises from 65/175
to 97/175 under client payloads (+18.3 percentage points, 95% CI 11.9 to 25.2) and from 71/175
to 132/175 with explicit fields (+34.9, 26.7 to
43.4). Gains concentrate in refusals (10/38 to
38/38 with explicit fields) and negated feelings
(2/32 to 13/32; Appendix Table 8). With explicit
fields the gate lets model text through on 62% of
requests (108/175); accepted outputs are fully correct in 71/108 and template fallbacks in 61/67 (Appendix B audits the failures); Appendix Figure 4
follows one selection through all four languages.

Limitations. The scorer is lexical and its Indic
lexicon is not native-reviewed; scenarios cover
one- and two-concept selections from a 69-symbol
board; one team wrote the rules, gold annotation,
and scorer without an independent curator; the
Hindi dialogues are machine translations; English
results are single samples; and no user study has
been run, so the results bound lexical faithfulness
and not communication effectiveness. Speech was
synthesized in placeholder mode, so latency (Appendix C) excludes synthesis and speech quality
was not assessed. Gujarati has no predictor and
Marathi reuses the Hindi trie, so extending Table 3
to both is the next phase and needs AAC dialogue
corpora for them. In the evaluated build the fallback template is not re-validated and the gate’s intent test covers English cues only.

Regression result. On the original 175 scenarios under version 2, the repaired hybrid and the
repaired template both score 175/175 with client
and explicit inputs (archived system: 95 and
138; matched one-pass QWEN2.5-0.5B-INSTRUCT
prompt: 8/175), and the hybrid exceeds the same
cascade without gate and fallback by 49.7 percentage points (95% CI 42.3–57.1). These scenarios
informed the repairs, so 175/175 is a regression
target and not a test result, and a lexical scorer cannot separate hybrid from template. A fresh set of
120 scenarios (30 per language) was generated after the freeze; its gold annotation awaits two native
speakers per language, so no score on it is reported,
and blind native ratings of meaning and phrasing
remain the pending test.

7

Conclusion

A small prompted language model can sit in front
of an AAC voice when a deterministic gate decides
what is spoken. Across 175 frozen scenarios in
four languages, the gate kept every selected concept and removed all polarity errors, while the template floor, which speaks only the user’s own labels,
matched or exceeded the pipeline under a lexical
scorer. The audit produced a repaired build that
now serves as a regression target; native-speaker
confirmation of a fresh scenario set, blind ratings
of meaning and phrasing, and real speech in all
four languages are the next steps toward measuring
what model phrasing adds.

Ethics Statement
An AAC device speaks for a person, so a plausible but wrong sentence can misstate consent or refusal. The gate lowers this risk without removing
6

it: it checks selected concepts, polarity, and a limited set of intent cues, not meaning in full. The
evaluated interface speaks a sentence as soon as
it is ready and does not show when a template replaced model output; the repaired build labels templates and does not speak a clarification or a literal
preview unprompted, but consequential messages
should still be confirmed with the user or a caregiver, and a preview-and-confirm step for generated sentences is a priority for deployments. Direct
Speak and Quick Express remain available when
generated text is unsuitable.
Voice cloning requires a reference clip and its exact transcript from the speaker or a guardian, with
consent; clones are held in server memory only.
Optional features can send typed words to third parties (Google Input Tools, OpenAI Images) or store
My Words and bookmarks in Firebase; generation,
prediction, and speech do not need them. The
evaluation uses synthetic dialogues and scripted
scenarios and contains no personal data; a study
with AAC users would need accessible consent and
the option to withdraw data. Accessibility testing
covered keyboard activation, emulated touch, and
automated checks of names and live regions, not
screen readers or switch scanning; the page has no
live region for new text and renders nothing when
the content delivery networks that serve its React,
Babel, and Tailwind libraries are blocked, although
generation and prediction run on the local server.

B Scorer Definitions and Failure Audit

Of the 37 accepted outputs that fail the scorer with
explicit fields, 35 are genuine failures (12 negated
want for a negated feeling, 10 ungrammatical, 9
third-person reports, 4 added content) and two are
marker-list gaps, as are all six failing template fallbacks and all eight failing template-only outputs
(the authors’ classification, not native-checked).

Retention is the share of required concept instances
present (lexicon forms with inflection stems). Polarity is correct when a negator is present exactly if
the gold message is negated, and the act is correct
when a request marker (want, need, चाहिए, જોઈએ,
हवे) or, for feelings, a feeling marker without a request marker is present. A concept is added if any
other board concept or a time, meal, or venue word
occurs. An output is off-task if it is a model refusal or meta-comment, a third-person report (“the
user…”), empty, longer than 25 tokens, or contains
Latin text in an Indic target; a user’s own refusal (“I
do not want water”) is never off-task. Failure audit.
