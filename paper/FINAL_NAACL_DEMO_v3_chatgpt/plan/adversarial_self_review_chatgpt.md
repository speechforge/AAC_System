# Adversarial self-review (author-side simulation)

1. **Does the hybrid beat a template?** No. The archived web policy is 12.0 percentage points below the matched template. Revised hybrid and repaired template both score 175/175 only on the known regression set. The abstract, results, and conclusion make no naturalness or generalization claim.
2. **Are 215/215 concepts and zero polarity errors a semantic safety guarantee?** No. Wrong-act, missing-act, added-content, perspective, and lexical-coverage errors are separated. All accepted outputs remain subject to unmeasured sense and phrasing errors.
3. **Were the new 120 scenarios independently validated?** No. The handoff explicitly prohibits publication of those scores before two native speakers per language confirm gold annotations. Those scores are absent from the manuscript and packaged numeric evidence.
4. **Does the demo actually speak all four languages?** Not established. All 32 playback runs used test tones; no IndicF5 weights or headless browser voices were available. The title and scope now concern symbol-to-sentence generation, and the paper distinguishes configured routing from synthesis evidence.
5. **Is the package submission-ready?** No. Public package/video, actual voice evidence, native review, author-specific CRediT allocations, and release permissions are unresolved. Karmakar and Sinha's real ACL paper has primary-record verification, but an independent index did not resolve it. These gaps are author notes, not fabricated results.

Strongest evidence: paired recorded-candidate comparisons with exact source counts and confidence intervals, followed by an audit of where automatic checks and scoring fail.
Highest reviewer risk: native-validated generalization and practical value beyond templates remain open.
