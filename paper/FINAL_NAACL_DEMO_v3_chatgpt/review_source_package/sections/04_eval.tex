\section{Evaluation and Analysis}
\label{sec:eval}
We first evaluate the archived policy on actual browser-built inputs, then use explicit gold fields to diagnose input interpretation.
The paired comparison measures the combined effect of the gate, retry, and fallback policy, not a gate in isolation.
We subsequently audit the scorer and report repairs only as a regression on the same grid.

\subsection{Protocol and Scoring}
The archived grid contains 184 monolingual scenarios: 46 selections in each of four languages.
Each language has ten requests, ten refusals, eight feelings, eight negated feelings, six action--object selections, and four help or call selections with a person.
Nine prompt-overlapping scenarios are excluded before the primary analysis, leaving 175: 46 English, 45 Hindi, 42 Gujarati, and 42 Marathi.
Eight Hinglish scenarios are exploratory and excluded from the primary counts.
The original text-generation run used one NVIDIA GTX 1650 with 4\,GB memory.

The systems are a literal label join, an unconstrained single-prompt Qwen diagnostic, the recorded cascade candidate before the gate, the archived returned output, and the archived template baseline.
The single prompt has no ledger, examples, verifier, or gate; its \Naive{} success is a diagnostic for that prompt, not a comparison with all one-pass prompting strategies.
For the policy comparison, pre-gate and returned outputs share the recorded response, holding the incoming selection fixed.
The \emph{browser} condition uses inferred act and polarity; the \emph{gold-field} condition supplies both explicitly and is a controlled diagnostic rather than a measured user choice.

Under archived \emph{scorer v1}, success requires every selected concept, the gold polarity and act, no detected added concept, and an on-task sentence in the target script.
Its lexical tests approximate these properties; they do not measure naturalness or certify meaning.
Concept retention counts required instances over 215, whereas success and error counts use 175 scenarios.
Errors overlap.
An off-task output is a model refusal to perform the task, third-person narration, an empty or overlong response, or inappropriate script leakage.
A valid user refusal such as \emph{``I do not want water''} is an intended message, not an off-task refusal.
We report 95\% paired percentile bootstrap intervals from 10,000 resamples of the 46 underlying message groups, keeping their language variants together.

\subsection{Actual Browser Inputs and Gold-Field Diagnosis}
The archived policy improves browser-input success from \WebCascade{} to \WebPolicy{}, a gain of 18.3 percentage points (95\% CI 11.9--25.2), but remains 12.0 points below the template baseline (CI $-17.9$ to $-6.4$).
This comparison includes browser interpretation.
The policy retains 215/215 required concept instances, compared with 175/215 before the gate, and has zero scored polarity errors.
Nevertheless, it has 54 wrong-act outputs and four added-content errors: preserving selected words alone does not preserve the complete message.

Explicit gold act and polarity fields raise policy success to \GoldPolicy{}, compared with \GoldCascade{} before the gate and \GoldTemplate{} for the template.
The policy gain over the cascade is 34.9 points (CI 26.7--43.4), while its deficit to the template is 20.0 points (CI $-26.4$ to $-14.0$).
Wrong-act outputs fall from 54 to 17 when gold fields replace browser inference.
The policy still has three added-content errors, and 37/108 accepted model outputs fail the full scorer; six of 67 template fallbacks also fail it.
Acceptance thus measures satisfaction of the implemented checks, not success under every scoring criterion.

\subsection{What the Audit Changes}
The interface diagnostic concerns 32 \emph{single-symbol feeling selections}, eight symbols across four locales, before prompt exclusions; it is not a count of the 175 scenarios.
The archived browser labels 27/32 as requests.
For example, a feeling can become an unwanted desire: \lang{Hindi} \bad{\hi{मुझे गुस्सा चाहिए।}} (\emph{``I want anger''}).
The same representational problem affects \lang{Gujarati} \gu{ગુસ્સો} and \lang{Marathi} \hi{रागात}: a feeling label needs a state construction rather than an object-request frame.
These two labels illustrate the board vocabulary, not new measured outputs.
After exclusion, 30 feeling scenarios remain in the primary grid.
The archived substring rule also falsely negates three of 276 single-symbol selections, including English \emph{Nose} and Gujarati \gu{નાક}.

The scorer audit separates system failures from lexical gaps.
All eight gold-field template failures are Gujarati negated feelings: the templates use \gu{નથી}, but scorer v1 does not recognize that construction as a feeling act.
The audit also classifies two accepted-model failures and six fallback failures as scorer gaps.
The other 35 accepted-model failures comprise 12 negated wants, nine third-person reports, ten structurally ill-formed outputs, and four additions; these are diagnostic classifications without native-speaker adjudication.
Consequently, the original 167/175 template score should not be interpreted as eight verified meaning errors.

The repairs address each layer explicitly.
Canonical symbols replace negation substring matching; act and polarity are separate; multilingual act and perspective cues strengthen the gate; and templates include negated feeling constructions such as \lang{Hindi} \hi{मैं खुश नहीं हूँ।}, \lang{Gujarati} \gu{હું ખુશ નથી.}, and \lang{Marathi} \hi{मी आनंदी नाही.}
These recorded regression outputs share the gloss \emph{``I am not happy.''}
Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} shows their decision phases alongside English, and Appendix~\ref{app:cases} samples the remaining scenario families.

\subsection{Repair Regression and Evidence Boundary}
With \emph{scorer v2}, which repairs lexical gaps and adds script, perspective, and act checks, the saved archived outputs score 95/175 on browser inputs and 138/175 with gold fields.
The repaired hybrid and repaired template each reach 175/175 on the same primary grid, while the repaired ungated first candidate reaches 88/175.
The hybrid's 49.7-point advantage over its ungated candidate has a paired group-bootstrap CI of 42.3--57.1 points.
These runs used one NVIDIA TITAN Xp with 12\,GB memory and English seed 11; Indic decoding is greedy.
The repaired browser and gold fields normalize to identical message representations on this grid.
Because the grid informed the repairs, these are \emph{regression results}, not evidence of generalization or a hybrid advantage over templates.
Scorer-v1 and scorer-v2 scores must not be mixed to estimate a repair effect.

A separate 120-scenario evaluation and a blinded template--hybrid rating packet were prepared.
Their gold annotations still await independent native-speaker confirmation, and no human ratings have been returned; we therefore report neither their automatic success rates nor a preference result.
Human judgments must evaluate meaning before phrasing.

\subsection{Next-Word Prediction}
Prediction uses a separate split of AAC dialogues: 240 training and 60 held-out source conversations per language for English and Hindi.
The test set supplies 2,220 English and 2,437 Hindi next-word contexts from AAC-user utterances.
All count-based baselines share the training partition.
Table~\ref{tab:pred} distinguishes the unigram baseline from the deployed suffix trie and marks the unmeasured languages.

The suffix trie obtains 381 correct top-one predictions versus 356 for trigram backoff in English, and 659 versus 630 in Hindi.
The count-derived gains are 1.126 and 1.190 percentage points, respectively, rather than differences between rounded table values (95\% conversation-bootstrap CIs 0.505--1.766 and 0.463--1.922).
Despite disjoint source conversations, 18/392 English and 28/384 Hindi test utterances recur in training.
Removing contexts from repeated utterances leaves gains of 0.968 points in English and 1.020 in Hindi.
Out-of-vocabulary targets, 167 English and 133 Hindi, remain misses.
No Gujarati or Marathi prediction result is inferred from the realization experiments.

\subsection{Playback and Installation Checks}
On the GTX 1650, 32 browser trials covered four languages, four selections, and two repeats, with one cold and 31 warm trials.
Click to the first audio-element playback event had median 9.9\,s and p95 20.8\,s.
The language medians were 12.4\,s (English), 15.3\,s (Hindi), 7.9\,s (Gujarati), and 10.7\,s (Marathi).
Every trial played a placeholder tone, so these are \emph{playback-path timings}, not click-to-first-audible-speech measurements.
A fresh CPU environment served example and clarification requests using cached Qwen weights; a first-time weight download, clean GPU installation, and real Indic synthesis were not tested.
Model execution is local to the backend, but the browser still depends on externally served interface libraries, so the complete demo is not verified as offline.
