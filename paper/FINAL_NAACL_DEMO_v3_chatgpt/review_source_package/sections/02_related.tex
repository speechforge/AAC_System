\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Grammatical expansion of telegraphic AAC input predates language models.
Compansion uses syntactic and semantic processing to expand abbreviated messages \citep{mccoy1998compansion}.
\citet{karmakar-sinha-2024-aiding} study bidirectional text--symbol communication and include Bengali translation.
These precedents motivate a narrower contribution: checking candidate realizations and measuring the consequences of acceptance and fallback in \lang{Hindi}, \lang{Gujarati}, and \lang{Marathi}.

Existing systems also differ in the information available before generation.
SpeakFaster expands abbreviated typed input for eye-gaze AAC users \citep{cai2024speakfaster}, while ConnecTone uses conversational context for text suggestions and style-adaptive speech \citep{francis2024connectone}.
In contrast, symbol selections provide an explicit set of concepts that our policy can check, although they may still leave the act ambiguous.

Jellow Basic already documents expressive-button sentence construction, pre-made sentences, and support for Gujarati, Hindi, and Marathi \citep{jellow}.
Table~\ref{tab:related} therefore distinguishes realization from a documented candidate check; an unstated capability is not evidence of absence.
We compare performance against matched baselines rather than infer superiority from product descriptions.
